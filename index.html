About

Marat Gukasyan

Marat Gukasyan is an artist based in London, UK. He works across mixed media and gilded sculpture, drawing inspiration from ancient and medieval art practices.

Essay

A Modern Medieval

By Marleen Boschen — curator, researcher and artist based in London

All-seeing eyes everywhere. A small mushroom cloud in the background. A motherly, or sisterly, figure is carefully holding a world in her belly. A village in ruins. We see all this in bold colours, ornately patterned. These abstract patterns turn into barbed wire and surveillance, post-Soviet landscapes fold inwards and dissolve like a feverish dream that holds the past and the future.

Taking the visual language of contemporary political and media messaging into a world where demons and spirits roam freely Marat's artworks ask: How do today's screen-mediated lives feel on the pages of a medieval manuscript? What might a modern medieval and its symbols look like in the war-fuelled and complicated year of 2024? At times liberating, at times sinister, often playful, these enchanted worlds are inhabited by shape-shifting forces that echo and distort classic archetypes: the witch, the priestess, the heroine, the sister.

Landscapes and minds ravaged by war appear across Marat's intricate drawings echoing medieval centuries but also evoking Russia's ongoing war on Ukraine. There's a sense of paranoia and suspicion that can be felt from the characters inhabiting these worlds – trust no one. Even the all-seeing eyes appear concerned and authoritative at once, the priestesses aren't fully relaxed.

There's a buzz in the air. The signal from the big bang continues to transmit, long gone moments in the past linger, an 80s TV on static. A flickering image of a pre-Christian Russia that carries older lived experiences of survival, brutal winter, endurance and sacrifice. In a spiralling present when we increasingly turn to astrology, tarot or the iChing for revelations, Marat's works embrace a different kind of divination. He summons a world where only female beings roam, part feminist sanctuary, part revenge fantasy. Here, ancient mythologies are rewritten to make sense of a densely saturated present, where white cats might tell secrets and technologies combust.

Commissions

Request a piece

Marat accepts a limited number of commissions each year, in gilded sculpture. Most projects begin with a short exchange about scale, subject, and material — the quality of the gold, the gesso, and the mounting of natural crystals — followed by a preliminary sketch or maquette before work begins. A deposit is required to confirm a commission; timelines vary by scale and typically run 8–20 weeks for sculptural work.
